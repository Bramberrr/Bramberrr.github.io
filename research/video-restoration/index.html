Decoupling Artifact Removal and Texture Recovery in Compressed Video

Zhiwei Xu, Gaurav Sharma, Thrasyvoulos N. Pappas

Northwestern University · University of Rochester

In preparation for Signal Processing: Image Communication, special issue on Extreme Super-Resolution

Overview

HEVC compression removes fine textures and adds blocking, ringing and mosquito noise. Restoration networks suppress these artifacts well, but they tend to oversmooth texture-rich regions. Adding a perceptual texture loss directly does not fix this, because textures and compression artifacts share similar high-frequency characteristics.

We decouple the two jobs. A conventional restoration backbone first removes the artifacts (stage 1). A lightweight refinement network then recovers perceptually relevant texture, using the compressed video as auxiliary guidance while constraining how far it moves from the stage-1 reconstruction (stage 2). We test it with three backbones (ShiftNet, BasicVSR++ and STDF) on 10 held-out 4K videos at HEVC CRF 32, 36 and 40.
